# The AI Infrastructure Signal
## [T.tokens at work](https://tokensatwork.com) · Issue #48 · November 22nd to November 28th, 2026

### Executive Summary
The week was defined by a sharp market correction driven by export control tightening and regulatory scrutiny, yet underpinned by resilient physical AI revenue growth. NVIDIA’s manufacturing partners halted shipments to Southeast Asia amid new US chip export controls, while the company itself paid $290 million to investors for concealing cryptocurrency mining revenue. Despite stock volatility, NVIDIA forecasts over $25 billion in Physical AI revenue, and AWS Trainium3 benchmarked favorably against the GB300 in inference tasks. In parallel, HPC and quantum computing advanced with TU Delft’s hybrid workflow demonstration and Quantinuum’s breakthrough in magic state distillation. The dominant tension remains between supply chain constraints and the accelerating demand for specialized compute architectures.

### Signal of the Week
NVIDIA’s manufacturing partners halted server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries, a move corroborated by reports from Developing Telecoms, Punch Newspapers, and Thaiger citing new US export rule enforcement. This development signals a hardening of geopolitical supply chain fractures that will constrain compute capacity expansion in key growth markets for the next 3-6 months, forcing enterprises to accelerate domestic fab reliance or pivot to alternative architectures like AWS Trainium3.

### Key Stories
- **NVIDIA** — Halts AI server shipments to Southeast Asia amid new US export controls
  - NVIDIA’s manufacturing partners stopped shipping servers to four Southeast Asian countries following stricter US chip export regulations.
  - This supply chain disruption risks slowing data center build-outs in high-growth regions, shifting investment focus toward domestic and allied-market fabs like TSMC in Texas.

- **NVIDIA** — Pays $290 million settlement for concealing cryptocurrency mining revenue
  - NVIDIA agreed to pay $290 million to investors after a report revealed it concealed cryptocurrency mining revenue in prior disclosures.
  - While the settlement highlights governance risks, it also underscores investor confidence in the company’s core AI data center business, which continues to drive growth despite regulatory headwinds.

- **AWS** — AWS Trainium3 outperforms NVIDIA GB300 in inference benchmarks
  - AWS Trainium3 demonstrated superior performance over NVIDIA’s GB300 in an inference benchmark published by Tokenpost.
  - This result suggests a viable alternative for inference-heavy workloads, potentially offering investors exposure to lower-cost, high-efficiency compute options outside the NVIDIA ecosystem.

- **NVIDIA** — Forecasts $25 billion+ in Physical AI revenue
  - NVIDIA projects more than $25 billion in revenue from physical AI applications over the coming period.
  - This forecast indicates a diversification beyond generic LLM training into robotics and industrial automation, creating a new growth pillar less sensitive to consumer GPU demand cycles.

- **AMD** — Opens Agentic AI for Supercomputing with HPC Assistant tool
  - AMD launched a new HPC Assistant to enable agentic AI workflows on its supercomputing infrastructure.
  - This move positions AMD as a strong alternative to NVIDIA in the scientific computing sector, particularly where export controls limit access to US-made chips.

- **Meta** — Pauses nonvoting share issuance, delaying $60 billion AI funding route
  - Meta paused the issuance of nonvoting shares, effectively delaying a planned $60 billion capital raise for its AI infrastructure expansion.
  - This pause may slow Meta’s internal compute build-out but could also signal a strategic shift toward more efficient capital allocation or partnership models.

- **Broadcom** — Velocity problem becomes new security challenge
  - Broadcom highlighted that high-speed data transmission now poses significant security risks, framing speed as a security challenge.
  - This insight suggests that future networking and chip designs must balance performance with robust security architectures, potentially impacting router and switch vendor strategies.

### Market Pulse
The market is currently navigating a bifurcation between supply-side constraints and demand-side innovation. On one hand, export controls targeting Southeast Asia and China are creating immediate bottlenecks in server availability, leading to a sell-off in major chip stocks like NVIDIA, AMD, and Broadcom. On the other hand, the sector is seeing robust fundamental growth signals, such as NVIDIA’s $25 billion Physical AI revenue forecast and AWS Trainium3’s competitive benchmarking performance. The dominant tension this week is whether these geopolitical headwinds will persist long enough to derail the broader AI infrastructure thesis or if they will simply accelerate a shift toward more resilient, diversified supply chains. Looking ahead 1-3 quarters, I expect continued volatility as investors digest the full impact of shipment halts, but the underlying capex trajectory for HPC and networking remains intact due to insatiable model scaling demands.

I’m confident that the AI infrastructure theme is structurally sound despite short-term noise; early call, but the direction is clear toward specialized hardware and sovereign compute stacks. The market is overreacting to regulatory headlines without fully pricing in the massive backlog of orders from government labs and cloud providers who have already committed multi-year contracts. However, this conviction hinges on the ability of vendors like NVIDIA and AMD to maintain their moats while navigating a fragmented global supply chain.

Two specific risks could break the current bullish thesis: first, if US export controls expand to include advanced packaging or memory from non-US allies, which would sever critical bottlenecks for even domestic fab operations; second, if Meta’s pause on share issuance cascades into broader tech sector capital discipline, reducing overall capex velocity in Q1 2027. The single most important thing to watch next week is whether NVIDIA clarifies its timeline for resuming shipments to Southeast Asia or announces a new regional manufacturing partnership that mitigates the export control risk.
