### Executive Summary
The AI infrastructure sector faces a sharp near-term correction driven by tightening US export controls, which have forced NVIDIA to halt shipments to Southeast Asia and led to an 8% stock crash despite record revenue. Simultaneously, the market grapples with NVIDIA’s $290 million settlement for concealing cryptocurrency mining revenue, further pressuring valuations. Conversely, significant capital commitment remains evident, including a reported $63 billion deal between Broadcom and Wall Street firms to power Anthropic’s hardware expansion, alongside SpaceX investing over $74 billion in supercomputer campuses. The dominant tension is the divergence between regulatory headwinds restricting market access and deepening financial commitments from major players like Meta, Microsoft, and Amazon to secure alternative compute pathways.

### Signal of the Week
NVIDIA announced a halt in AI server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries amid new US chip export controls, a move corroborated by reports from Developing Telecoms and multiple financial outlets citing immediate stock declines for NVIDIA, AMD, and Broadcom. This development signals a critical inflection point where regulatory restrictions are physically constraining supply chains just as demand for high-bandwidth memory and advanced GPUs peaks, potentially forcing the market toward alternative suppliers like MediaTek or domestic US foundries. The specific risk is that these export curbs will accelerate the shift in capital allocation toward non-US geopolitical entities, while the opportunity lies in companies developing compliant architectures or serving markets outside the restricted zones. (High confidence on the immediate supply shock; medium confidence on long-term market fragmentation).

### Market Pulse
We are in a phase of defensive consolidation where regulatory overhangs are overshadowing growth narratives, creating a bifurcated market between restricted entities like NVIDIA and opportunistic players like AMD or domestic foundries. The dominant tension this week is the clash between physical supply chain disruptions—NVIDIA halting shipments to Southeast Asia—and financial commitments that remain robust, such as Broadcom’s $63 billion deal for Anthropic. This means we should expect continued volatility in chip stocks over the next quarter as investors digest the implications of export controls while looking for yield in alternative hardware or software-defined compute solutions.

I'm confident on the thesis that capital will flow toward non-restricted architectures and domestic energy hubs, but early call, though the direction is clear toward diversification away from single-vendor reliance. The conviction lies in the fact that total addressable market for AI infrastructure is expanding faster than regulatory headwinds can contract it, provided companies adapt their supply chains quickly enough to bypass geopolitical choke points.

The two specific risks that would break this thesis are a broader escalation of US export controls targeting not just Southeast Asia but also Europe or India, and a failure of alternative suppliers like MediaTek to deliver compliant chips at scale within the next 12 months. The single most important thing to watch next week is whether NVIDIA provides further clarity on its compliance timeline for existing orders and if any new partners announce non-US manufacturing agreements to mitigate these risks.
